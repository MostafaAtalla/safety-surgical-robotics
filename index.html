Safe for which tissue?

Nearly every surgical robot is motivated by safety, yet the word rarely arrives with a hazard, a condition or a measurement attached. This Working Group asks what safety means technically, how it is evaluated, and what evidence a safety claim should carry.

Why this group

One word, many measurements, no shared standard of evidence.

Studies support safety with force, pressure, stiffness, speed, accuracy, failure tolerance or controllability. Each measure is relevant and none is sufficient, because its meaning depends on the procedure, the anatomy, the task, the operating condition and the failure mode in question. Two papers can both report a “safe” robot and share no comparable quantity.

The group grew out of the ICRA 2026 workshop Soft ≠ Safe? Rethinking Softness in Medical Robotics, which showed that even a property as intuitive as softness does not settle the question. The scope is deliberately wider: rigid, hybrid and soft systems; sensing and control; human–robot interaction; autonomy; and behavior under faults.

01

Clarify terms

How safety, compliance, robustness, backdrivability and safe failure are used across communities.

02

Identify dimensions

Physical interaction, mechanical design, sensing, control, autonomy, human operation and failure behavior.

03

Compare evidence

The measures, experimental tests and assumptions currently used to support safety claims.

04

Sharpen claims

State what is claimed, under which conditions it holds, and what evidence supports it.

05

Connect practice

Bring clinical and industrial experience into the academic discussion.

What the group does not do. It does not certify devices, replace regulatory testing or write a formal standard. It works earlier, where researchers still choose their definitions, tests and evidence.

Leadership

WG co-chairs

Five co-chairs and a founding senior advisor from four institutions. Each activity has one lead, with the others supporting where their expertise is needed. Every annual workshop includes at least one co-organizer and one invited speaker from North America and from the Asia-Pacific region.

    Programme

    Four activities over a two-year term.

    Annual

    Workshop

    One workshop a year at a RAS-sponsored conference such as ICRA, IROS, RoboSoft or BioRob. Each takes one question: the evidence a safety claim needs, failure behavior, softness and safe interaction, or safety under shared and autonomous control. A public summary records agreements, disagreements and open questions.

    4–6 episodes

    Expert interviews

    Short recorded interviews with researchers, clinicians and engineers. Each answers one focused question: which test is most informative, or which failure mode receives too little attention.

    Year 1

    Community survey

    How do you define technical safety, which measures and tests do you use, and which terms or evaluation gaps cause the most difficulty? Circulated internationally through RAS TCs, mailing lists and professional networks.

    Year 2

    Synthesis & publication

    A public perspective or review paper mapping the main views and gaps, and a proposed special collection with an IEEE RAS journal such as T-MRB, T-RO or T-SRO.
